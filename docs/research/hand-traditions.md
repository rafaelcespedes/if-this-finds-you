# Hand traditions: where real handwriting variety comes from

_Research for Dynamic Font, 2026-11-02. Purpose: decide which handwriting traditions to trace as letter skeletons,
from which public-domain items, and which structural letter variants (allographs) each one adds._

## Summary

- **Schooling is the best axis for variety.** How someone writes is set mostly by the model they were taught, where
  and when. A seed should pick a *tradition* first (say, a Polish copperplate school hand of the 1900s), then that
  tradition's letters, then a few personal quirks. TypeTogether's **Primarium** maps 71 current school models in 41
  countries ([primarium.info](https://primarium.info), CC BY-SA 4.0): the best reference for *what* each country
  teaches. Its Playwrite fonts (OFL) are for checking structure, never for tracing.
- **Nearly every modern school model is in copyright** (German LA, SAS, VA and Grundschrift; Swiss Basisschrift;
  Comenia; Falski's Polish *Elementarz*; French ministry fonts; Rubio; D'Nealian; Nelson; all modern Nordic models).
  The clean sources are **historical copybooks and writing masters**, **two official models published as state
  documents** (Austria 1969, Vietnam 2002), and **ordinary people's writing** under open licences.
- **HTR ground truth is the fastest source of ordinary hands**: datasets with line-aligned transcriptions under CC BY
  4.0, such as 146 French WWI soldiers (Testaments de Poilus) and 267 present-day Québec volunteers (Copiste d'un
  jour). A script can pull every "g" from hundreds of real writers.
- **Library terms matter as well as copyright.** Gallica (commercial reuse licensed), BNE (NC), MDZ's Google scans
  (NoC-NC) and Arolsen restrict use even of public-domain works, and aggregators show stale labels. Open copies of
  the same works usually exist: Rijksmuseum, the Met, MDZ's own scans, Getty copies on the Internet Archive,
  Houghton, NB.no, Polona, JCyL.
- **§4 lists about 120 allographs** and the bundles they travel in (continental 1-with-flag + crossed 7 + crossed z).
- **Diacritics are written differently by hand**: Polish cursive ł has its bar *on top*; the Czech háček is one
  swoosh for every letter, placed *beside* ď and ť; Hungarian ő is more open than ö; Vietnamese tone marks drift right.
- **Gaps:** Africa, Southeast Asia beyond Vietnam, Latin America after 1900, Hungary, Romania, Greece. For these,
  consented contributions with local partners (roadmap Phase 4) are more honest than tracing colonial copybooks.
- **Recommended v1: 13 traditions** (§8), all clean or low-risk, across 10 languages and three hand-ages.

**Rights shorthand used throughout:** **Clean** (public domain, open copy), **Caution** (an issue to resolve: NC
terms, share-alike, a jurisdiction where it is still protected, or an unconfirmed official-works claim), **Avoid**
(in copyright, a licensed font, or ethically excluded). Items marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be checked on a
primary page. Not legal advice.

**Codes used in the tables.** CP copperplate/English roundhand, SP Spencerian, PAL Palmer, ZB Zaner-Bloser, DN
D'Nealian, FR French cursive, ES Spanish bastarda, IT Italian chancery and *corsivo*, KU Kurrent, SÜ Sütterlin, LA
Lateinische Ausgangsschrift, SAS Schulausgangsschrift, VA Vereinfachte Ausgangsschrift, GS Grundschrift and other
print-first models, MR Marion Richardson and the Nordic *formskrift* derived from it, PL Polish, CZ Czech/Slovak,
HU Hungarian, VN Vietnamese, AR/BR/CU/PT Argentina, Brazil, Cuba, Portugal.

### 1.13 Future expansion: Cyrillic and Greek

**Cyrillic: feasible, well sourced.** Russian cursive allographs: т like m (optional bar above), ш with an optional
bar below, д as ∂, ambiguous minim letters (и л м ш щ ы); Serbian and Macedonian cursive differ in г, п, т (with
macrons), б and Д ([Wikipedia: Russian cursive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cursive)). Soviet monoline
model from the 1960s; Bulgarian slightly rounder ([Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/articles/modern-handwriting-a-historical-survey-5)).
PD items: *Прописи для скорописного почерка*, 1860, NLR scan, PD-RusEmpire ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Прописи_для_скорописного_почерка,_1860.pdf));
*Курс каллиграфии и конторской скорописи*, 1915, anonymous ([IA](https://archive.org/details/kurskalligrafiiikontorskojskoropisi60)).
Pre-1918 items include ѣ і ѳ. Soviet *propisi* of the 1950s–80s name authors: **Avoid**.

**Greek: feasible, thin sources.** Cursive β, ε, θ, μ, ν/υ, ρ, ς, φ differ from print (UNVERIFIED in a school
source); only NC or niche copybooks were found.

## 4. Allograph taxonomy

An **allograph** is a structurally different way to build a letter: a different skeleton, not a different size,
slant or pen. This is the build list for skeleton variants, with stable IDs for the code. "Traditions" says where a
variant is taught or typical; any adult can drift into any variant, which is what the seed's quirk budget is for.
Main sources: [Wikipedia: Regional handwriting variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_handwriting_variation),
[BYU Script Tutorial](https://script.byu.edu/german-handwriting/alphabet/gothic-handwriting/animated-lowercase),
Primarium, the Playwrite model notes on [Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/playwrite-fr-trad/about), and
the regional sources in §1.

### Which variants travel together

A seed should pick a **tradition first**, then letters from its bundle, then two or three off-bundle quirks.
Strongly correlated bundles:
- **Continental numerals:** 1.2 + 7.2, often with z.2 and Z.1. Almost never 1.2 with a plain 7.
- **Kurrent:** a.3, e.3, h.3, k.4, m.2, m.4, r.4, s.3/s.4, u.2, x.2, ß.1, KU.caps. All or nothing, except that
  u.2 and ß.1 survive into the Latin hands of people schooled before about 1941.
- **French:** p.3, r.2, s.5/s.6, v.2, f.5, z.3 or Z.2, m.3 lead-ins; upright to slight slant; round; Seyès proportions.
- **Italic** (Arrighi, MR, Nordic formskrift, Iceland, VA): b.2, d.2, f.2, k.3, l.2, r.3; unlooped; semi-joined.
- **American business** (PAL, ZB): r.1, k.1/k.2, q.1, z.3, I.1, Q.1, F.1, 2.2; plain 1 and 7.
- **Polish:** b.3, p.2, g.2, q.3, the special ł (§5); upright in the Falski era, slanted before it.

## 5. Diacritics and language-specific letters in handwriting

Two general rules. In joined hands, accents, i-dots, t-bars, x-crosses and the bar of ł are usually **delayed
strokes**: the writer finishes the word, then goes back, which matters for the stroke animation. And handwritten
accents are far less regular than typeset ones: angles collapse toward vertical or horizontal, acute and grave
merge into one short stroke in fast hands, and marks drift right in the direction of writing. So the mark library
should store a few *intentions* (rising stroke, falling stroke, cap, cup, dot, ring, hook, comma, wave) with
per-writer angle and offset, not one fixed glyph per accent.

## 6. Rights

*Research, not legal advice.*

### 6.1 Three layers

| Layer | Question | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| **The work** (copybook, letter, plate) | Public domain? | EU, UK, Turkey, Brazil, Russia: life + 70. US: published before 1931. EU anonymous works: 70 years from publication. Outliers (from memory, verify): Mexico life + 100, Côte d'Ivoire + 99, Colombia + 80, Spain + 80 for authors who died before Dec 1987, Vietnam and the Philippines + 50 |
| **The scan** | A new right in the photo? | US: no, for faithful 2D copies (*Bridgeman v. Corel*, 1999). EU: no, since Art. 14 of the 2019 DSM Directive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_on_Copyright_in_the_Digital_Single_Market)) |
| **The library's terms** | A contract on download? | Separate from copyright and sometimes enforceable: Gallica (paid commercial licence), BNE (NC), MDZ Google scans (NoC-NC), Arolsen. Prefer CC0/PDM copies of the same work |

We copy **letter structure**: a skeleton redrawn from a plate is a new drawing of the letter's design, not a copy of
the scan. That weakens the terms layer but doesn't remove it. **Policy:** trace from CC0, PDM or "no known
restrictions" copies wherever one exists, and store the source URL and rights statement with every skeleton.

### 6.2 Is a letter's structure protected?

A school handwriting model is, legally, close to a typeface.

| Jurisdiction | Typeface design | Font software |
|---|---|---|
| **US** | Not copyrightable: "typeface as typeface" is excluded (37 CFR 202.1(e)); *Eltra v. Ringer*, 579 F.2d 294 (4th Cir. 1978) ([text](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F2/579/579.F2d.294.77-1188.html)); calligraphy treated the same. Design patents possible | Protected as a program (*Adobe v. Southern Software*, 1998, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Systems,_Inc._v._Southern_Software,_Inc.)), though contested since about 2018 ([Butterick](https://matthewbutterick.com/chron/the-copyrightability-of-fonts-revisited.html)) |
| **EU** | **Design right**: "typographic typefaces" are named as products in the design definition, kept by Regulation 2024/2822 (applies from 1 May 2025) ([Casalonga](https://www.casalonga.com/actualites-et-documentation-pi/modeles/modele-francais-218/actualites/designs-models-package-key-changes.html?lang=en)). Unregistered: 3 years; registered: up to 25. Copyright only for the author's "own intellectual creation" | Software copyright |
| **Germany** | The 1981 **Schriftzeichengesetz** implemented the 1973 Vienna Agreement, giving typefaces design protection; now in the Designgesetz (§ 61 carries old registrations over). At most 25 years ([SchrZAbkG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/schrzabkg/BJNR203820981.html), [§ 61 DesignG](https://lexetius.com/DesignG/61)) | Software copyright |
| **UK** | Artistic work; ordinary typing and printing don't infringe (CDPA [s.54](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/54)); **free to copy 25 years after the typeface was first marketed** ([s.55](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/55)) | Software copyright |

### 6.3 What we may do

| Action | Risk |
|---|---|
| Trace a pre-1931, author-dead-70-years copybook from a CC0/PDM scan | **Clean** |
| Read the *rules* of a modern model (loops, slant, proportions) from Primarium or a ministry guide, cite them, and draw our own skeleton from those rules | **Clean** in the US; **low** in the EU/UK (only a registered design, an unregistered design under 3 years old, or a highly original letterform would bite) |
| Trace a PD work from an NC-licensed scan (BNE, Gallica, ONB) | **Caution**: swap for an open copy |
| Trace from CC BY-SA images (Europeana 1914–18, Paolo Villa, Commons photos) | **Caution**: share-alike *may* attach to skeleton data. Keep these in a separate attributed file |
| Trace letter by letter from a modern printed workbook | **Caution** in the EU/UK (decorative capitals may pass the originality bar), low in the US |
| Trace or convert outlines from a digital font (Playwrite, Grundschrift, Comenia, ministry fonts) | **Avoid**: OFL derivatives must stay OFL and can't be sold alone; Grundschrift is non-commercial; French ministry fonts reportedly ND |
| Name a hand after a commercial model (D'Nealian, Zaner-Bloser, Rubio, Comenia) | **Avoid**: trademarks. Name hands by place and period |

**Risky cases:** Marion Richardson in the US (probably restored to 2031); Falski's *Elementarz* (to 2045); modern
German LA/SAS/VA and Swiss Basisschrift; Comenia; Hungary's 1939 model; Vietnam 2002 and Austria 1995 (official-
works claims unconfirmed); anything from Gallica in a commercial product.

**Two questions worth a lawyer's hour:** (1) does share-alike attach to skeleton coordinates traced from CC BY-SA
images? (2) the URAA status of European works published 1927–1930 that we want to ship in the US.

## 7. Ethics

1. **Hands are people, not costumes.** Label a hand by schooling and period ("schooled in Lyon in the 1950s"), never
   as "the French style". Never exaggerate a tradition's features to make it read as foreign: that is the logic of
   faux-ethnic *simulation typefaces* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_typeface)).
2. **Show the lineage.** Every hand gets a card with its tradition, source item, library, date, rights and the
   writer or master where known. Credit women and children by name (Maria Strick, Anna and Elisabeth Waser, Catherine
   Godfrey, Toni Edelmeier) to balance a canon of male writing masters.
3. **Take letterforms, never words.** Don't display source text (a soldier's will, a prisoner's diary).
4. **Sensitive collections.** Exclude Arolsen. Don't use records of enslaved, incarcerated or colonised people
   (Freedmen's Bureau, convict records, Māori service records, Mapuche land records) as skeleton sources; their
   writers didn't write for posterity. If Indigenous material is ever involved, follow community protocols such as
   Local Contexts TK Labels.
5. **Living people.** Avoid writing after about 1950 unless the writer consented (the Copiste d'un jour and
   CREMMA-Wikipedia volunteers did).
6. **Kurrent and Sütterlin without kitsch.** They were everyday German writing until the Nazi ban of 1941. Present
   them as ordinary hands, not "old Germany" blackletter styling, which carries far-right associations today.
7. **Colonial legacies are now local identities.** Vietnamese *chữ thảo*, Nigerian Nelson cursive and Indonesian
   *huruf sambung* came through colonial schooling but belong to those cultures now. Cite local sources and have
   native writers review before shipping; a Vietnamese reader sees at once whether tone marks look right.
8. **Meaning-bearing marks are not decoration.** Turkish ı/i, Hausa ɓ ɗ ƙ, Yoruba subdots and Vietnamese tones
   change meaning. The quirk budget must never drop them.
9. **New scripts need native reviewers** before they ship (Cyrillic, Greek, Gaelic, and any non-Latin script).

otherwise Latin hand.

**Method.** A lead researcher and four parallel research agents (German-speaking, Dutch and Nordic; Romance and
beyond Europe; UK, US, Central Europe and Cyrillic; ordinary hands). The shared web-search budget ran out partway,
so most rights were checked against institutions' own records and APIs (MDZ IIIF manifests, Rijksmuseum linked
data, the Met API, Internet Archive metadata, NB.no, the Czech Digital Library, Europeana's record API, Wellcome,
Zooniverse, Commons file pages). Sites behind bot walls are marked UNVERIFIED. Main secondary sources: Primarium
(CC BY-SA 4.0) and Sébastien Morlighem's survey for Typotheque
([part 3](https://www.typotheque.com/research/modern-handwriting-a-historical-survey-3),
[part 4](https://www.typotheque.com/research/modern-handwriting-a-historical-survey-4),
