# All letters: browsing controls (research and the three options)

Rafael's brief: the letters are the main attraction; the controls take as little room as possible. Search may
start collapsed, era could be a different kind of control, letters vs notes may not need one, Shuffle can be an
icon, and a floating bottom toolbar like the tool's right panel could be nice.

## What current gallery and collection apps do (Mobbin, Nov 2026)

- **Apple Photos (iOS 18+) and Google Photos:** the grid runs edge to edge. A floating pill at the bottom holds
  the few views, and search is a separate round button beside it. Filtering is one small menu, not a row of fields.
  [Apple Photos](https://mobbin.com/screens/e7215100-cdc1-4799-9b86-f88dffba4441),
  [Google Photos](https://mobbin.com/screens/138b3aeb-138a-4d7a-a386-3883b28ce079)
- **Krea:** a masonry of results with one floating "Search all assets" pill over the bottom of the grid; the few
  other controls are icons, top right. [Krea](https://mobbin.com/screens/14fd2b80-7d7e-4bb4-b504-a5629977763a)
- **Savee (iOS):** a floating icon-only dock (home, search, add, saved) over a masonry; search opens at the top.
  [Savee](https://mobbin.com/screens/598ae442-e0a4-4923-adf0-d01febd58e42)
- **Cosmos:** one centred search pill in the header that opens into a panel of suggestions (colours, recent);
  no filter bar at all. [Cosmos](https://mobbin.com/screens/9843e150-828f-4db1-a007-badd11951c2d)
- **Luma, Higgsfield, Savee (web):** floating bottom bars appear for the action of the moment (prompt, selection),
  and stay out of the grid otherwise.

What they share: the grid gets the page; one or two controls are always in reach; everything else folds into a
menu or a search; icon buttons carry tooltips; counts appear only when they help (after filtering).

## The library's controls (all three options)

- **Letters vs notes: dropped.** Only 3 of 123 are notes, and search still finds them ("note").
- **Language: in search, and a light menu where there's room.** Search already matches language names
  ("Spanish", "French").
- **Era: lighter.** Plain words with a small chevron (a native menu underneath, so phones get their own picker),
  or, in #3, the timeline itself.
- **No Shuffle and no count on screen** (Rafael, after the first round). Screen readers still hear the count after
  each search or filter change. A letter at random stays on the letter page (its "More" menu).
- **Search: collapsed** to an icon until opened (`/` opens it; Esc clears, then closes).

## The options (`?toolbar=1…3`, kept for the tab, never in share links)

1. **In the heading.** A search icon at the right of "All letters"; under the intro, one sentence that is also
   the filter: "Letters from *any time* in *any language*".
2. **Floating dock.** A small floating bar at the bottom, the same surface as the tool's right
   panel: search (opens in place), and Era and Language menus. Rises above the phone keyboard.
3. **Timeline dock** (the default, Rafael's pick: "the kind of control that adds delight"). The same floating bar with search and a timeline instead of menus: every letter is a bar,
   grouped by century (Earlier, 1600s, 1700s, 1800s, 1900s), so you see when they were written; tap a century to
   see its letters ("Letters from the 1800s ×"). Language through search.
