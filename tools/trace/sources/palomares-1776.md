| Movement | A tall, narrow oval hand at about 14° (capitals nearer 16°), written with a broad nib: heavy downstrokes, hairline upstrokes. Small letters start with a short hairline hook at the x-height (i m n r u v) or a flag at the top of the ascender (b d h k l), and leave with a rising hairline from the baseline that reaches the next letter. Joins are partial and natural (`joins: natural`): letters keep the plate's own entries and exits and sit as close as on the plate, so a c d e h i k l m n t u x z leave on a rising hairline that reaches the next letter, while b o r v w finish at the top, s on its terminal and f g j p q y ſ in their descenders, and lift. Plain unlooped ascenders and descenders; f, ſ and y sweep long tails left under the previous letters; q has a flat foot; s has teardrop terminals. Capitals open with a curled hook and a long hairline swash over the top (A, E, F, K, T, X), and close with a flat baseline swash (E, L, Q, Z) |
| Note | Binarized at a fixed threshold (0.70 of the flattened page) so the show-through from the reverse leaf drops out while the hairlines survive. e, k, r, s, t, v, y, E, T and W were drawn over the plate by hand where the broad nib merges strokes the skeleton cannot separate. The tilde on the plate (õ ũ ã ñ ẽ) is a flat, heavy bar with a small down-turned hook at its left end, set well above the letter, about as wide as it; acute and grave are short straight ticks, the circumflex a small pointed caret |
