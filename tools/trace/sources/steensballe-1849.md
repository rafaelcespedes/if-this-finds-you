| Movement | A very steep English roundhand: the plate's own slant guide reads 45°; the skeletons keep 26° (the writer's slant adds to it). Pointed-pen shading, heavy downstrokes and hairline upstrokes. Small letters are entered by a long hairline rising from the baseline (u i t l b h k j) or by an overturn hairline from mid-height (n m v w r, and v and w begin like n); the connector meets the next letter at half the x-height (measured lead-in angle about 41°). Ascenders are plain, unlooped: the hairline climbs inside the stem and the stem comes down shaded (b d h k l t); descenders loop (g j y ſ) or stand straight (p, f, q). b, v and w close with a small knot at the x-height and leave from it; o leaves over the top; f leaves along its crossbar; q has a crossed descender and lifts after it; s leaves from its pointed top, its bowl closed by a ball. p has a half-ascender stem (p.3), x is two back-to-back curves with ball ends, z is a flat Z with a wavy top. Capitals are flourished roundhand forms: ball lead-ins (A, M, X), bottom-left curls (B P R T F), inner spirals (D O E), looped descenders (G J Y), a crossed Z; they do not join |
| Note | Credit the book to Brandt, the writing master named on the title page; P. F. Steensballe was its publisher. Binarized at a fixed threshold (0.90 of the flattened page) to keep the faint hairlines. The x-height ratio follows the plate (0.45 of the ascender); the engine's hand sampler currently sets its own x-height for non-`script` styles |
| Review | Coordinator review: ascender and descender limits tightened (lowerTop 1.055, descRatio 0.47, capital depth 0.5) so the tallest f and the deepest g, G, J, Y stay on the page for every seed |

## Kurrent (Gothic hand), 1849 — style `kurrent-1849` ("Kurrent 1849", joined)

| | |
|---|---|
| Item | Brandt, writing master (Skrivelærer) at the Borgerskole in Christiania, *Skjønskrivningsregler for de engelsk-latinske og gothiske eller norske Haandskrifter, med oplysende Exempler i otte lithographerede Plader*, Christiania: P. F. Steensballe, 1849. The plates of the Gothic ("gothisk eller norsk") small letters, grouped by basic stroke, and Gothic capitals; the Gothic example words on the Exempel 3–4 plate were read to confirm C and P and the joins |
| Copy | Nasjonalbiblioteket (National Library of Norway), URN:NBN:no-nb_digibok_2016101928012: https://www.nb.no/items/URN:NBN:no-nb_digibok_2016101928012 (images 0021 = Gothic small letters, 0023 = Gothic capitals; 0013 = Gothic example words) |
| Rights | `isPublicDomain: true`, licence `publicdomain` on the NB.no catalogue record; IIIF licence https://www.nb.no/lisens/#public-domain, which allows copying, changing and commercial use without asking (checked 2026-11-02). Published 1849, public domain everywhere |
| Traced | a (two forms: hooked stem a.3, and a second with a small loop at the stem head), b c d e f g h i j k l m n o q r s t u v w x y z, long s ſ, and ø (o with a tick above, as written in the Gothic hand). Capitals C M N O P Z (KU.caps). Small letters drawn over the plate by hand; capitals by waypoints snapped through the ink. Not registered: the plate's large final s (its bowl hangs below the line), because the engine picks one s per hand regardless of word position, so `s` is the round s and `ſ` the long s. Not on the plates: p, ß, æ, digits, punctuation; the other 20 capitals were not traced (their identity on the plate could not be confirmed with the example words). All of these borrow from `norwegian-1849` (Latin roundhand, same book) and then `script` |
| Used in | `tools/trace/hands/kurrent-1849.json` → `engine/sources/kurrent-1849.js` |
| Movement | Very steep (the plate's minims stand at about 45°; the skeletons keep 24°, the writer's slant adds to it) and angular: n m u e i r are pointed minims joined foot-to-head by long straight hairlines, so the whole word is a zigzag of shaded downstrokes and thin diagonals. e is a narrow n (two close minims), u is an n with its bow (breve) written after the word, i has its dot set high and to the right, r is a minim with a short second minim and a top hook, t has a pointed head with a retraced stem and its cross-stroke runs on as the connector. Joins sit high, at 0.8 of the x-height (measured lead-in angle about 25°). Ovals (o a c g q) are very narrow, entered over the top; o leaves from a knot at the top, v and w close with a curve and leave from the foot. Ascenders are tall loops (b d h k l f; d curls back over the letter); descenders are long: h, ſ, j, g, y, z and x loop back up to the right, f stands straight with its cross-stroke looped round the stem, q lifts after its straight descender. Capitals are pointed-pen Kurrent forms with oval lead-ins, knots and baseline loops; they do not join |
| Note | Credit the book to Brandt, the writing master named on the title page; P. F. Steensballe was its publisher. Binarized at a fixed threshold (0.90 of the flattened page). Legibility is 'historical' by design: these are Kurrent forms, not Latinised. The plate's x-height is about a third of its ascender; the engine's hand sampler sets its own x-height for non-`script` styles (about 0.6 of cap height), so the units use x-height 0.42 and let ascenders rise to 1.22 to keep the tall loops |
| Review | Coordinator review: lowerTop 1.03 and descRatio 0.45 so the tall looped ascenders (d b h l) and long descenders (h x y z j) stay on the page; the loops are a little shorter than on the plate. Capitals other than C M N O P Z, and p, ß, æ, borrow the Latin forms of the same book (`norwegian-1849`) |
