Recording your hands

One take gives the video everything: your face from the laptop's camera, your hands from above on your phone, and every key and pointer move from the recorder page. Setting up takes about ten minutes the first time.

1The phone, above the keyboard

  • About 50 cm (20 inches) above the keyboard, looking straight down. An overhead phone stand, a tripod with a sideways arm or a clamp-on arm all work; so does a shelf.
  • Sideways, with the top of its picture toward the screen, so your hands come in from the bottom.
  • In the picture: the whole keyboard, the whole trackpad, both wrists and a little of the desk in front. Nothing cut off.
  • Steady: tap the desk while you watch the phone. If the picture shakes, brace the stand or move it off the desk.

2The phone's settings

  • On an iPhone, Settings › Camera › Record Video: 1080p at 60 fps (or 4K at 60), HDR Video off, and Lock Camera on if you have it, so it doesn't switch lenses mid-take.
  • In the Camera app: Video, 1×. Press and hold on the keyboard until AE/AF LOCK shows. If your hands look too bright, drag the sun down a little.
  • No zoom, no Cinematic or Action mode.

3Light and desk

  • Even light on your hands, from the front or the side: daylight, or a lamp bounced off a wall. No hard shadow across the keyboard.
  • Keyboard backlight off. It dims and brightens as you type, which flickers on camera. Control Center › Keyboard Brightness, all the way down.
  • Sleeves above the wrists. A watch or rings are fine; they'll show in the corner video.

4The Mac

  • Open “Typing recorder.html” in Google Chrome: drag it onto Chrome, or right-click it › Open With › Google Chrome.
  • Volume at about 70%, nothing in your ears. The phone listens for the page's beeps to line up with it. Turn on Do Not Disturb, so no other sounds come in.
  • Click “Turn on the camera” and allow the camera and the microphone. Sit as you usually do, with the screen at its usual angle, and check you're in the little preview.

5The take

  • Start the phone recording first.
  • Press Start on the Mac. Three beeps, then a higher one.
  • Look at the camera for a moment (that's the opening of the video), then look down and write.
  • Write as you would on the site. ⌫ crosses out what's written (what the pen hasn't reached yet just disappears); keep pressing to scribble it out. ⌘B goes over words, ⌘U underlines, ⌘Z lifts ink that's still wet. Click or drag with the trackpad to move the caret or select words; typing over a selection writes the new word above.
  • Keep your hands in the phone's view the whole time, and don't lean in so far that your head hides the keyboard.
  • Click Finish at the top right when you're done. Two beeps. Then stop the phone.

6Sending it

  • The take saves itself to Downloads as “Typing take date at time.zip”.
  • AirDrop the phone's video to the Mac. It lands in Downloads too. Send it whole, untrimmed.
  • Tell Claude they're there.

First, a test take

  • About 30 seconds: a sentence or two, one typo fixed with ⌫, one click into the middle of a word with the trackpad, one drag across a word and ⌘U.
  • That shows whether the tracking holds up with your hands and your light, before you write the real letter.

Then the letter

  • Keep it short: under a minute of typing reads best in a post. Longer works too; the middle can be sped up.
  • Do as many takes as you like. Each one saves its own file.
If something goes wrong. The camera won't turn on: check Chrome is allowed to use it (System Settings › Privacy & Security › Camera), or record the take without it and film your face with Photo Booth at the same time. You make a mess of a take: click Finish, then New take. Chrome asks where to save: pick Downloads.
